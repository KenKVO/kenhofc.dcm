**Abstract.** Parametric response mapping (PRM) is a voxel-based quantitative CT biomarker that uses paired inspiratory and expiratory scans to phenotype COPD (emphysema vs. functional small-airway disease, fSAD). Accuracy depends on scanner type and protocol. We developed 10 human chest models with emphysema and air-trapping at end-inspiration and end-expiration, imaged them with DukeSim for energy-integrating and photon-counting CT, and compared PRM maps with ground truth. Emphysema volume was overestimated by 3±9.5% of lung volume; fSAD was underestimated by 7.5±19%. Measurements were more accurate and precise with PCCT, higher dose, smoother kernel, and larger pixel size.
