**Abstract (journal).** Clinical imaging trials are costly, slow, and ethically constrained. Virtual imaging trials (VITs) simulate patients, scanners, and readers in silico. VLST, inspired by the National Lung Screening Trial (NLST), asked whether a VIT platform could recover key elements of that CT-versus-chest-radiography (CXR) lung-cancer screening trial.

A cohort of 294 XCAT human models with simulated cancerous nodules underwent CT and CXR. Deep-learning AI CT-Reader and AI CXR-Reader acted as virtual readers to recall patients with suspected lung cancer. Primary outcome: difference in diagnostic performance (AUC). The AI CT-Reader achieved AUC 0.92 (95% CI 0.90–0.95) versus 0.72 (0.67–0.77) for the AI CXR-Reader. At the NLST-reported 94% CT sensitivity, VLST specificity (73%) matched NLST specificity (73.4%).

**Conference version (SPIE 2024).** *Virtual NLST: towards replicating the National Lung Screening Trial.* Sixty-six computational phantoms with simulated nodules were imaged with an NLST-like Duke Legacy W20 CT model in DukeSim. A LUNA16-trained virtual reader (3D RetinaNet + ResNet-10 false-positive reduction) exceeded 95% sensitivity at fewer than 3 false positives per scan on clinical and virtual CTs; sensitivity was higher for nodules ≥10 mm.
