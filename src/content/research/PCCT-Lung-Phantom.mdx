**Abstract.** Photon-counting CT (PCCT) can improve spatial resolution and dose efficiency relative to energy-integrating CT, but protocol-only patient studies cannot isolate those effects for quantitative COPD biomarkers, and existing phantoms lack realistic pathology. We combined a digital lung-disease model with a PixelPrint 3D-printed anthropomorphic lung phantom containing airways, vessels, mucus, nodules, and emphysematous parenchyma. The phantom was scanned in Normal-Resolution and Super-High-Resolution modes across CTDI$_\mathrm{vol}$ levels and kernel strengths. Quantitative endpoints included mean lung density, LAA-950, Perc15, wall thickness, WA%, mucus volume, and nodule volume, using fixed segmentation for cross-setting comparison. Super-high-resolution with stronger iterative reconstruction gave the best spatial fidelity and quantitative reproducibility across biomarkers.

Journal manuscript in preparation (Ho FC, Mei K, Sharma S, et al.).
